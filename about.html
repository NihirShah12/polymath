About

Fifteen minutes, every morning.

A small daily act of replacing the scroll with something that lasts.

The Polymath Dispatch began as a personal experiment: instead of opening a feed every morning, open one well-made lesson. One thinker or one chapter of history at a time, taken slowly and in order, so the pieces build on each other.

The two rooms

Philosophy follows sixteen thinkers — Marcus Aurelius and Epictetus, Nietzsche and Jung, Baldwin, hooks, Lorde and Fanon, Freire and Campbell, Watts, Ram Dass and Thich Nhat Hanh, Zukav, Brené Brown and Gabor Maté — through their lives, their central arguments, their critics and their relevance now.

History runs strictly in order: India from prehistory to the present day, then Europe from Greece and Rome to the modern age, and finally a full block on the Industrial Revolution.

How each dispatch is made

Each lesson is researched from scholarly and reference sources, drafted with the help of AI, then checked against those sources. Every link is a real source; every video comes from YouTube's own records; every image is freely licensed from Wikimedia Commons and credited. Established facts, contested interpretations and speculation are kept clearly apart, and where historians disagree, the main positions are named.

It is a learning journal, not an authority. If something matters to you, follow the sources and check it.
